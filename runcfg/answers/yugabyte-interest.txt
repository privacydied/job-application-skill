Two things.

The first is that this is support for a distributed database, which means the tickets are
genuinely diagnostic rather than procedural. I have spent a long time on the other side of
that: I run a self-hosted Linux homelab — Docker services, monitoring, automated backups,
hardening — and I have run production infrastructure at CryptoKnowledge, where I owned
hosting, CI/CD, security hardening and backups for the platform I had built. I also ran a
self-hosted LynxChan and MongoDB stack behind Cloudflare as a deliberate performance exercise,
which is where a lot of my instinct for caching, replication behaviour and measuring before
and after came from. So a problem that turns out to be a network partition, a slow query plan
or a misread consistency setting is the kind of problem I want to be given.

The second is the users. Two years of end-user break-fix at Comfix taught me how people
actually describe faults, and five years of user research taught me to listen for what they
have not said yet. Support for a database is mostly working with engineers who are already
frustrated and already half-diagnosed — the job is to take their theory seriously, verify it,
and be straightforward when it is wrong. I would rather do that than hand over a template.

I am also comfortable in the open: Yugabyte being open source means the answer is often "read
the source and the issue history", which is how I already work.
