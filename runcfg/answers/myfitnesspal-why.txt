Two threads in my work lead here.

The first is health at scale. I spent two and a half years as an interaction designer on
the NHS COVID-19 App at UKHSA, working inside the GOV.UK Design System under real
accessibility constraints and cross-government scrutiny. That taught me what it means when
a health product has to work for everybody — not the median user, everybody — and what it
costs when it doesn't.

The second is behaviour change. At Gramrphone I mapped onboarding against real feedback
from musicians and cut onboarding time roughly fourfold, with downloads-to-installs
throughput up around sixfold. None of that came from a redesign; it came from watching
where people actually gave up. A tracking app lives or dies on the same question — the
product only works if logging today is easy enough that someone still does it in week six.

MyFitnessPal sits exactly where those two threads meet: a consumer health product at large
scale where the design problem is sustained habit, and where accessibility is a
correctness requirement rather than a nice-to-have. I'm also a builder — Node.js, Postgres,
Docker, CI/CD — so I'd rather work close to the implementation than hand off a file and
hope.
