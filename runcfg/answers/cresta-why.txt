Cresta is applying AI to a setting where the person using it is under real pressure and the
quality of the assistance is measured in whether it actually helped — not in whether the
demo was impressive. That's the part I want to work on.

My background is a designer's, six years of it, with five years of hands-on user research.
The lesson I keep returning to is that people describe their problems in their own terms,
not the system's, and that most of the value in an assistive tool is in the gap between
those two. Contact-centre work is that problem in its sharpest form: the agent has seconds,
a customer mid-sentence, and no appetite for a tool that needs interpreting.

I'm also hands-on with the technology rather than adjacent to it. I built and self-host a
multimodal Discord assistant built around tool use and an agentic command loop, and
wl-dictate, which runs Whisper and a local llama.cpp model together on-device. I use
agentic tooling daily as part of how I work. So I have a reasonably concrete sense of where
these systems are genuinely good, and where they confidently produce something wrong — which
matters more when a human is expected to act on the output immediately.
