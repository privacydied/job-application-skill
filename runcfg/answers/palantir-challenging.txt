The NHS COVID-19 App, during my two and a half years as an interaction designer at what is
now UKHSA.

The hard part was not the interface. It was that the product had to work for everyone in the
country, under time pressure, while the underlying epidemiological guidance kept changing —
and that a person misreading a screen had real consequences, not a bad conversion rate. We
worked inside the GOV.UK Design System with accessibility as a hard requirement, across a
multidisciplinary team and several government bodies, at a pace where a decision taken on
Monday could be live by Friday.

Two things from it stayed with me. The first is that clarity is a safety property: if a
screen can be misread under stress, that is a design defect, and no amount of visual polish
compensates. The second is that constraints from other disciplines — clinical, legal,
epidemiological — are not obstacles to design around but the actual shape of the problem. The
design work that mattered most was often reconciling those constraints into something a
person could act on in fifteen seconds.

Outside that, the build I am proudest of is gearo, a price-comparison engine for music gear
that I own end to end: the product and UX, a Node.js and Postgres back end, and a multi-source
scraping and normalisation pipeline that dedupes noisy listings across twelve-plus
marketplaces and ranks them by relevance, geography and price context. The design problem
there is almost entirely a data problem wearing an interface.
