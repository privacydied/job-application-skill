The thing I find genuinely interesting about Palantir's framing is that the hard problems are
rarely the technical ones. They are institutional: the data exists, the analysis is possible,
and the obstacle is that the people who need to act cannot see it in a form they trust or
understand in time.

I have worked at that seam. On the NHS COVID-19 App the underlying epidemiology was not the
bottleneck — the bottleneck was turning it into something a person could act on immediately,
correctly, without training, across the whole population. Getting that wrong is not a usability
inconvenience; it is a public-health outcome. That is the same category of problem as helping
an analyst or an operator make a consequential decision from complicated data under time
pressure.

What I would want to work on is the part where a system's model of a problem meets a human
who has to be accountable for the decision. Interfaces for expert users under pressure are
their own discipline: the user is not confused, they are busy and responsible, and the design
job is to be legible and honest about uncertainty rather than to simplify it away.

I am also a builder rather than only a specifier — Node.js and Postgres, a self-hosted Linux
homelab, CI/CD and infrastructure I run myself — so I am comfortable working close to the
engineering rather than handing off a specification.
